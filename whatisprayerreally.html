What is prayer, really?

Let me clear two misunderstandings off the table first, because they exhaust more praying people than any devil does.

Prayer is not information. You are not briefing heaven. “Your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask him” (Matthew 6:8). Nothing you say to God arrives as news, and this is not a discouragement to asking; it is the removal of performance from it. You are not required to say it well. You are required only to say it to Him.

And prayer is not persuasion, the wearing down of a reluctant God by volume and repetition, as though the Father had to be argued into what the nails already purchased. The cross settled the question of His willingness before you were born. When you pray, you are not knocking on a locked door; you are speaking inside a house whose door was opened at the highest possible cost.

What is it, then? Prayer is communion. It is the meeting of persons, the way the first man knew the voice of God in the garden in the cool of the day. It is less like filing a request and more like the well I stood at as a boy: deep water, really there, waiting to be drawn.

“But the hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him.”John 4:23 · KJV

Why then does it run dry for so many of us? Usually not because the spring failed. In most lives prayer is there, stopped with the packed earth of busyness and a hundred small screens. Wells do not die; they silt. And silted wells are not cursed, they are dug again, the way Isaac reopened the wells of his father and found the water still living.

So begin small and honest. Ten unhurried minutes with no screen in reach. Read a few verses slowly, not to finish them but to listen; the Book is His side of the conversation. Then speak plainly, the way the psalmists did, who complained and rejoiced and feared and hoped in the same breath: “As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth my soul after thee, O God” (Psalm 42:1). He is not grading your grammar. He is receiving His child.

And when you fail at this, and some weeks you will, do not resign. Come back. Come back today, come back tomorrow, until coming back is simply the way you walk. The Father you are learning to meet has one standing sentence over all your arrivals: “him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out” (John 6:37).

This answer is drawn from A Genuine Fellowship: The Cross Radius, where it has a whole chapter to breathe.
