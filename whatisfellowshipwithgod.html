What is fellowship with God?

The apostle John, an old man by then, who had leaned on Jesus at supper, wrote down the reason for everything he preached in one sentence:

“That which we have seen and heard declare we unto you, that ye also may have fellowship with us: and truly our fellowship is with the Father, and with his Son Jesus Christ.”1 John 1:3 · KJV

Not: our doctrine is with the Father. Not: our duty, our tradition, our attendance. Our fellowship. The word means shared life, common ground, the thing two friends have and two strangers do not. John was claiming acquaintance with the living God, and he was inviting his readers into the same.

Here is the difference I have watched for years, in my own chest before anyone else’s. Religion is transport; fellowship is acquaintance. Religion can move you along the road, in the company of God’s people, at a blameless pace: baptized, attending, serving, respected. And a person can ride that road for decades in the company of a Rider he has never actually met. The heart, in its honest hours, knows whether it is riding with a stranger.

The two disciples on the Emmaus road walked beside the risen Jesus for miles and did not know Him, until He broke the bread and their eyes were opened, and they said one to another, “Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way?” (Luke 24:32). That burning is what religion without acquaintance never feels, and what fellowship cannot forget.

How does such acquaintance begin? Not by climbing. The whole gospel is the story of God closing the distance Himself: the Maker of all things sitting wearied on a well’s rim, asking a woman for a drink, offering her living water at the exact coordinates of her daily thirst. Fellowship begins where honesty meets that offer: a person finally speaking to God as someone actually there, and letting Him answer through His Book, His Spirit, and His people.

And it grows the way any acquaintance grows: time, truth, and shared road. Unhurried prayer that is meeting rather than performance. Scripture read as listening. Obedience walked out as company kept, not tax paid. Fellowship among His people, where His voice so often carries. None of it is exotic. All of it is water.

If you have suspected that there is more to know of God than you have known, that suspicion is not your accuser. It is your invitation. “O taste and see that the LORD is good” (Psalm 34:8). A menu can be memorized; a meal must be eaten.

This answer is drawn from A Genuine Fellowship: The Cross Radius, where it has a whole chapter to breathe.
